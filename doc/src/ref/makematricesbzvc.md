# makematricesbzvc

## Usage
- `makematricesbzvc[op, bz]` gives the list `{{qn, mat}, ...}` of the matrices `mat` of the operator *op* in the invariant subspaces of the basis *bz*, labeled by their quantum numbers `qn`; the basis states are in the occupation number representation.

## Details
- A basis in SNEG is a list `{{qn1, states1}, {qn2, states2}, ...}`. Each element describes one invariant subspace (symmetry sector): `qn` is the list of its quantum numbers and `states` the list of its basis states. `bzQ` tests for this form.
- The states are either occupation number vectors `vc[n1, n2, ...]` (functions with names ending in `vc`) or operator expressions, strings of creation operators acting on the vacuum (functions ending in `op`, and the basis generators without `vc`). `bzvc2bzop` and `bzop2bzvc` convert between the two forms.
- In `vc[n1, n2, ...]`, $n_k \in \{0, 1\}$ is the occupation of the k-th single-particle state of the global list `BASIS`, set by `makebasis`. For spin-1/2 fermion levels $c_1, c_2, \ldots$, the order is $c^\dagger_{1\uparrow}, c^\dagger_{1\downarrow}, c^\dagger_{2\uparrow}, \ldots$, and `vc[n1, n2, ...]` is the state $(c^\dagger_{1\uparrow})^{n_1} (c^\dagger_{1\downarrow})^{n_2} \cdots |0\rangle$.
- Bases are generated by the following functions, which also call `makebasis`:

| `qszbasisvc[{c[1], ...}]` | $\{Q, S_z\}$ | charge and spin projection |
| `qsbasisvc[{c[1], ...}]` | $\{Q, S\}$ | charge and total spin; only the states with $S_z = S$ |
| `qbasisvc`, `szbasisvc`, `sbasisvc` | $\{Q\}$, $\{S_z\}$, $\{S\}$ | a single quantum number |
| `nonebasisvc` | `{}` | the whole Fock space as a single subspace |

- The charge is measured from half filling, $Q = N - N_\mathrm{levels}$, where $N$ is the number of electrons and $N_\mathrm{levels}$ the number of spin-1/2 levels. `transformQStoIS`, `transformtoLRvc`, `transformtoPH`, and `transformbasis` derive further bases with isospin, parity, phonons, or projections.
- `makematricesbzvc[op, bz]` gives, for each subspace, the matrix $M_{ij} = \langle i | \hat O | j \rangle$, where $|i\rangle$ and $|j\rangle$ run over the states of the subspace, in the order of `bz`. The matrices are simplified with `Simplify`.
- For an orthonormal basis and an operator that commutes with the symmetry generators (a Hamiltonian with the symmetry of the basis), the matrices are the blocks of the block-diagonal operator. The full spectrum is the union of the eigenvalues of all blocks; in a $(Q, S)$ basis, every eigenvalue in the subspace $(Q, S)$ is $(2S+1)$-fold degenerate. The eigenvector $(v_1, v_2, \ldots)$ of a block is the state $\sum_i v_i |i\rangle$, obtained as `v . states`.
- Matrix elements between different subspaces are not computed. For operators that change the quantum numbers, such as $c^\dagger_{i\sigma}$, use `makeallmatricesbzvc`.
- `makematricesbzvc` returns unevaluated if *bz* is not of the form tested by `bzQ`.
- The states `vc[...]` refer to the current value of `BASIS`. Calling a basis generator for another set of levels redefines `BASIS`, and the old states are then no longer valid: operators of levels that are not in `BASIS` are not applied (see `matrixrepresentationvc`), and products of such operators lead to infinite recursion. Generate the basis again with `makebasis` or the basis generator before computing matrices.

## Developer Notes
- `makematricesbzvc` maps `matrixrepresentationvc[op, states, states]` over the subspaces. Operators are applied to the states with `ap`. For large bases, `matrixrepresentationvcfast` is faster.
- `examples/diagonalize.m` contains routines for exact diagonalization, thermodynamic traces, and spectral functions built on `makematricesbzvc` and `makeallmatricesbzvc`; NRG Ljubljana (`nrginit`) constructs its initial Hamiltonian in the same way.

## Examples
### Basic Examples
Basis of the Hubbard dimer with well-defined charge $Q$ and total spin $S$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[t, U];
bz = qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}]
```
Matrices of the Hamiltonian $H = -t \sum_\sigma (c^\dagger_{1\sigma} c_{2\sigma} + \mathrm{h.c.}) + U \sum_i n_{i\uparrow} n_{i\downarrow}$ in the subspaces $(Q, S)$:
```wl
H = -t hop[c[1], c[2]] + U Total[hubbard[{c[1], c[2]}]];
mats = makematricesbzvc[H, bz]
```
Eigenvalues by subspace. At half filling ($Q = 0$), the lowest state is the singlet with $E_0 = (U - \sqrt{U^2 + 16 t^2})/2$:
```wl
{First[#], Eigenvalues[Last[#]]} & /@ mats
```

### Scope
A basis with quantum numbers $(Q, S_z)$ contains all members of the spin multiplets; the subspaces are larger than in the $(Q, S)$ basis:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
{First[#], Length[Last[#]]} & /@ qszbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}]
```
```wl
{First[#], Length[Last[#]]} & /@ qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}]
```
---
`makematricesbzop` gives the same matrices for the basis in the creation operator representation:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[t, U];
H = -t hop[c[1], c[2]] + U Total[hubbard[{c[1], c[2]}]];
bz = qszbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}];
makematricesbzvc[H, bz] === makematricesbzop[H, bzvc2bzop[bz]]
```
---
`showbzmat` displays the matrices:
```wl
showbzmat[makematricesbzvc[H, bz]]
```
---
Spin chain: projecting the three-site basis onto single occupancy with `transformbasis` gives the Hilbert space of three spins 1/2. Heisenberg chain $H = J (\mathbf S_1 \cdot \mathbf S_2 + \mathbf S_2 \cdot \mathbf S_3)$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[J];
bz = transformbasis[qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2], c[3]}], nc @@ projector1[{c[1], c[2], c[3]}]];
{First[#], Eigenvalues[Last[#]]} & /@ makematricesbzvc[J (spinspin[c[1], c[2]] + spinspin[c[2], c[3]]), bz]
```

### Applications
Anderson impurity model with a single bath level (two-site Anderson model) at the particle-hole symmetric point, $H = U (n_{d\uparrow} - \frac12)(n_{d\downarrow} - \frac12) + V \sum_\sigma (d^\dagger_\sigma c_\sigma + \mathrm{h.c.})$, written up to a constant as $-\frac{U}{2} n_d + U n_{d\uparrow} n_{d\downarrow}$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[d, c];
snegrealconstants[U, V];
H = -U/2 number[d[]] + U hubbard[d[]] + V hop[d[], c[]];
bz = qsbasisvc[{d[], c[]}];
mats = makematricesbzvc[H, bz];
{First[#], Eigenvalues[Last[#]]} & /@ mats
```
The ground state is the singlet $(Q, S) = (0, 0)$ with $E_0 = -(U + \sqrt{U^2 + 64 V^2})/4$; the lowest triplet state has $E = -U/2$. To second order in $V/U$, the splitting is the Kondo exchange $J = 8 V^2/U$:
```wl
Series[-U/2 + (U + Sqrt[U^2 + 64 V^2])/4, {V, 0, 2}, Assumptions -> U > 0]
```
Ground state for $U = 1$, $V = 1/4$ in the occupation number representation; the eigenvectors are sorted by energy:
```wl
{vals, vecs} = Eigensystem[N[keyselect[mats, {0, 0}][[1, 2]] /. {U -> 1, V -> 1/4}]];
gs = vecs[[First[Ordering[vals]]]] . keyselect[bz, {0, 0}][[1, 2]]
```
Impurity occupancy, double occupancy, and the spin correlation $\langle \mathbf S_d \cdot \mathbf S_c \rangle$ (−3/4 for a pure singlet):
```wl
Chop[expvvc[{number[d[]], hubbard[d[]], spinspin[d[], c[]]}, gs]]
```
---
Thermodynamics of the Hubbard dimer: in the $(Q, S)$ basis the partition function is $Z = \sum_{Q,S} (2S+1) \sum_i e^{-\beta E_i^{(Q,S)}}$. At $\beta = 0$, $Z$ is the dimension of the Fock space, 16:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[t, U];
mats = makematricesbzvc[-t hop[c[1], c[2]] + U Total[hubbard[{c[1], c[2]}]], qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}]];
Z[beta_] := Total[(2 #[[1, 2]] + 1) Total[Exp[-beta Eigenvalues[#[[2]]]]] & /@ mats];
{Z[0], Z[1] /. {t -> 1, U -> 4} // N}
```

### Properties & Relations
For each subspace, the matrix is `matrixrepresentationvc` with the states of the subspace:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[t, U];
H = -t hop[c[1], c[2]] + U Total[hubbard[{c[1], c[2]}]];
bz = qszbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}];
makematricesbzvc[H, bz] == ({First[#], matrixrepresentationvc[H, Last[#]]} & /@ bz)
```
---
The dimensions of the subspaces add up to the dimension $4^2$ of the Fock space; in the $(Q, S)$ basis each state counts with the multiplicity $2S+1$:
```wl
{Total[Length[Last[#]] & /@ bz], Total[(2 #[[1, 2]] + 1) Length[Last[#]] & /@ qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}]]}
```
---
`makeallmatricesbzvc` also gives the blocks between different subspaces and omits vanishing blocks. For a Hamiltonian with the symmetry of the basis, only diagonal pairs of subspaces remain (here without the subspaces in which $H = 0$):
```wl
First /@ makeallmatricesbzvc[H, bz]
```

### Possible Issues
Matrix elements between different subspaces are dropped. A transverse magnetic field $B S^x$ on a single level does not conserve $S_z$, and `makematricesbzvc` gives zero matrices instead of the eigenvalues $\pm B/2$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[d];
snegrealconstants[B];
bz = qszbasisvc[{d[]}];
makematricesbzvc[B spinx[d[]], bz]
```
The off-diagonal blocks are given by `makeallmatricesbzvc`:
```wl
makeallmatricesbzvc[B spinx[d[]], bz]
```
---
A $(Q, S)$ basis contains only the states with $S_z = S$. For operators that are not spin-rotation invariant, such as the Zeeman term $B S^z$, the matrices only describe these states; the levels with $S_z < S$ are missing. Use a $(Q, S_z)$ basis instead:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[B];
makematricesbzvc[B (spinz[c[1]] + spinz[c[2]]), qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}]]
```
