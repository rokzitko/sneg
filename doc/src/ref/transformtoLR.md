# transformtoLR

## Usage
- `transformtoLR[bz, map]` transforms the basis *bz* in the creation operator representation into a basis with an additional quantum number, the parity $P = \pm 1$ under the mirror reflection defined by the list of levels *map*.
- `transformtoLR[bz, map, rules]` applies the additional substitution *rules* together with the reflection.

## Details
- *bz* is a basis `{{qn, states}, ...}` (see `makematricesbzvc`) whose states are operator expressions, as generated by `qsbasis`, `qszbasis`, or `bzvc2bzop`. The result has the same form, with the parity appended to each list of quantum numbers, e.g. $(Q, S) \to (Q, S, P)$.
- The reflection $\hat P$ is defined by `lrmap[map]`: the levels in *map* are mapped onto the levels at the mirror positions, `map[[k]]` ↔ `map[[-k]]`. For a state $f(c^\dagger)|0\rangle$, $\hat P f(c^\dagger)|0\rangle$ is obtained by substituting the creation operators in $f$; the vacuum is even. Fermionic signs from reordering the operators are taken into account.
- For each subspace with states $|i\rangle$, the even and odd combinations $(|i\rangle \pm \hat P |i\rangle)/2$ are orthonormalized within the span of the subspace (Gram-Schmidt); the resulting states have $P = +1$ and $P = -1$. Empty subspaces are removed, and the result is sorted by the quantum numbers.
- Each subspace of *bz* must be mapped onto itself by the reflection. This holds for the charge $Q$, the spin $S$ and $S_z$, and the isospin, provided all levels exchanged by the reflection are in the basis.
- The optional *rules* describe the transformation of further degrees of freedom under the reflection, e.g. `{ket[k_] :> (-1)^k ket[k]}` for a phonon mode that is odd under reflection, or a sign change of orbitals with odd parity.
- In a system with mirror symmetry, the Hamiltonian commutes with $\hat P$ and is block-diagonal in the parity basis. For an impurity coupled symmetrically to two leads $L$ and $R$, the even combination $(c_L + c_R)/\sqrt{2}$ couples to the impurity and the odd combination $(c_L - c_R)/\sqrt{2}$ decouples; the parity basis separates the corresponding subspaces.
- The calculation is done in the creation operator representation and is slow beyond two sites (about 15 s for three levels in a $(Q, S)$ basis). `transformtoLRvc` performs the same transformation and returns the basis in the occupation number representation in a fraction of this time.

## Developer Notes
- Do not supply occupation vectors as the input states: substitution of the site permutation in a `vc` state does not permute its bits and can silently classify every state as even. Convert with `bzvc2bzop` first.
- The steps are: substitution with `lrmap[map]` and *rules*, the combinations with `mapbasis`, orthonormalization with `orthogbasisop` (i.e. `orthogop` for each subspace), `dropemptysubspaces`, and `appendquantumnumber`. NRG Ljubljana uses `transformtoLRvc` for parity-adapted bases of two-channel models (`lrchain` and `lrextrarule` in the model files).

## Examples
### Basic Examples
Basis of the Hubbard dimer with quantum numbers $(Q, S, P)$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[t, U];
lr = transformtoLR[qsbasis[{c[1], c[2]}], {c[1], c[2]}]
```
The Hamiltonian is block-diagonal; all blocks except the even singlet at half filling are $1 \times 1$. The bonding (even) and antibonding (odd) one-electron states have the energies $\mp t$:
```wl
makematricesbzop[-t hop[c[1], c[2]] + U Total[hubbard[{c[1], c[2]}]], lr]
```

### Scope
Basis with $(Q, S_z, P)$ quantum numbers:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
{First[#], Length[Last[#]]} & /@ transformtoLR[qszbasis[{c[1], c[2]}], {c[1], c[2]}]
```
---
Two levels coupled to a phonon mode that is odd under the reflection, $b \to -b$; the phonon states $|k\rangle$ transform as $(-1)^k |k\rangle$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
bz = transformtoPH[qsbasis[{c[1], c[2]}], {1}];
lr = transformtoLR[bz, {c[1], c[2]}, {ket[k_] :> (-1)^k ket[k]}];
{First[#], Length[Last[#]]} & /@ lr
```

### Applications
Single-level impurity `d` coupled symmetrically to two leads `a` and `b` with one site each, $H = \frac{U}{2}(n_d - 1)^2 + V \sum_\sigma (a^\dagger_\sigma d_\sigma + b^\dagger_\sigma d_\sigma + \mathrm{h.c.}) + \epsilon (n_a + n_b)$. The parity-adapted basis is constructed with `transformtoLRvc`, which is faster. The largest subspace is reduced from 8 to 4 states:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[a, d, b];
snegrealconstants[U, V, eps];
bz = qsbasis[{a[], d[], b[]}];
lr = transformtoLRvc[bz, {a[], d[], b[]}, vacuum[]];
H = U/2 isozsq[d[]] + V (hop[a[], d[]] + hop[b[], d[]]) + eps (number[a[]] + number[b[]]);
{Max[Length /@ bz[[All, 2]]], Max[Length /@ lr[[All, 2]]]}
```
The impurity couples with $\sqrt{2} V$ to the even orbital; the odd lead orbital, with energy $\epsilon$, is decoupled. For one electron in the odd orbital and none elsewhere ($Q = -2$, $S = 1/2$, $P = -1$), the impurity is empty, and the energy is $\epsilon + U/2$:
```wl
mats = makematricesbzvc[H, lr];
keyselect[mats, {-2, 1/2, -1}]
```
The eigenvalues in the subspaces $(Q, S)$ agree with the calculation without parity:
```wl
num = {U -> 1, V -> 3/10, eps -> 1/10};
spec[m_] := Sort[Flatten[Map[Function[x, {Take[x[[1]], 2], #} & /@ Eigenvalues[N[x[[2]] /. num]]], m], 1]];
s1 = spec[mats]; s2 = spec[makematricesbzvc[H, bzop2bzvc[bz]]];
{Length[s1], s1[[All, 1]] == s2[[All, 1]], Max[Abs[s1[[All, 2]] - s2[[All, 2]]]] < 10^-12}
```

### Properties & Relations
The states have definite parity: applying the reflection to an odd state changes its sign:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
lr = transformtoLR[qsbasis[{c[1], c[2]}], {c[1], c[2]}];
st = keyselect[lr, {0, 1, -1}][[1, 2, 1]];
{st, st /. lrmap[{c[1], c[2]}]}
```
---
`transformtoLRvc` gives the same basis in the occupation number representation:
```wl
Expand[bzop2bzvc[lr]] === Expand[transformtoLRvc[qsbasis[{c[1], c[2]}], {c[1], c[2]}, vacuum[]]]
```

### Possible Issues
The Hamiltonian must be symmetric under the reflection. A site energy on one site only breaks the symmetry; the matrix elements between the even and odd subspaces are dropped, and the one-electron energies come out as $\epsilon/2 \mp t$ instead of $(\epsilon \mp \sqrt{\epsilon^2 + 4t^2})/2$:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
snegrealconstants[t, eps];
H = -t hop[c[1], c[2]] + eps number[c[1]];
lr = transformtoLR[qsbasis[{c[1], c[2]}], {c[1], c[2]}];
{Flatten[Eigenvalues /@ Cases[makematricesbzop[H, lr], {{-1, 1/2, _}, m_} :> m]], Eigenvalues[keyselect[makematricesbzop[H, qsbasis[{c[1], c[2]}]], {-1, 1/2}][[1, 2]]]}
```
---
The basis must be in the creation operator representation. For occupation number vectors, the substitution has no effect, and all states are classified as even:
```wl
snegfermionoperators[c];
First /@ transformtoLR[qsbasisvc[{c[1], c[2]}], {c[1], c[2]}]
```
